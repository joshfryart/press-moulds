# Equation playground presses sound moulds, hides silent traps

For a polymer-clay earring maker working at 20-40 mm, the app's geometry is sound and its feedback is not. The model is a watertight 2.5D heightmap and the mould is a drafted, undercut-free single block. On the tested designs, release along the press direction is guaranteed by construction, and all 20 test equations built watertight (code-run measurements). The risks are mostly about trust. Several controls accept input that the output then ignores (strand height under draft, surface amplitude, the base plate, the loop on surfaces). The thin-resin-wall check reads 0.1-0.2 mm optimistic, so true gaps of 0.5-0.6 mm pass a 0.6 mm warning. A negative loop shift silently cuts a near-vertical wall. For this user the highest-value work is a day or two of guardrails and honest checks, plus a half-page of pressing and release guidance. Shrinkage compensation is cheap to add but irrelevant to FIMO Soft, which shrinks negligibly, and only matters if the user returns to earthenware (about 6.4% total, scale about 1.068). The code assessments rest on running the code. The two external-benchmark notes are search-snippet level and cannot settle any numeric design rule.

## What works: the geometry is exact where it counts

The model stage turns a parsed equation into centreline polylines, rasterises them into a per-pixel height grid with a domed strand profile capped by a draft cone, and triangulates the grid ([geometry.py](file:///home/user/press-moulds/equation-playground/geometry.py)). The strand profile is continuous in distance to the centreline, not binary. Ray-cast measurements against the analytic profile gave a maximum height error of **0.037 mm at the default 0.05 mm pixel** and 0.08 mm at 0.1 mm. Footprint width came out at 1.800 mm against a nominal 1.8 mm, and quadric decimation to the 400k-face cap left volume unchanged (90.5 mm³) (measured, [model_generation.md](file:///home/user/press-moulds/research_notes/Press%20mould%20and%20model%20assessment/model_generation.md)). Cusps, crossings, poles and zero-amplitude degenerate curves all stayed watertight. Because the strand is a distance field, corners get round joins for free. The parser is hardened too: a whitelist, a 500-character cap, guards on constant powers, and tests for pathological input. Hostile inputs such as `9^9^9 + x` were rejected in 0.00 s.

The mould inherits the same property. The cavity depth is a function of (x, y), so no undercut can exist, and the draft cone caps every wall at 20 degrees off the pull direction. Across 15 built designs the steepest pixel step was exactly the draft angle, with zero violations (measured, [mould_generation.md](file:///home/user/press-moulds/research_notes/Press%20mould%20and%20model%20assessment/mould_generation.md)). Crossing strands merge into a flat junction at the same 1.2 mm height, which is safe to demould but looks merged, not woven. Pair mode gives a mirrored left/right cavity set, which is exactly what earrings need, and the 4 mm of zero-padding plus margin leaves a flat land of 0.8 mm that a blade can shave against. A 0.8 mm-deep gutter ring and four vents per cavity were achieved in every run (0, 1, 4, 8 and 12 requested gave 0, 1, 4, 8 and 12 placed). Pins registered with 0.199 mm radial clearance against a nominal 0.15 mm, and the third pin is deliberately larger so the lid cannot close backwards.

At earring scale the numbers are comfortable. A 30 mm design builds in 9-18 s using 2.2-2.9 GB at the default pixel, and 6.7 s and 1.2 GB at 0.1 mm. A pair block runs about 93 x 54 x 4.5 mm and uses about 20 ml of resin per part, roughly 42 ml per mould-and-lid set. These figures sit inside the app's own "draft about 5 s, final 10-25 s" spinner text. The auto hanging loop kept hang tilt under 2 degrees on every tested shape, and the loop hole stayed tangent to the form so no strand was cut (14 of 14 designs, 1 body, draft intact at 20 degrees). The 127-test suite passes in 23 s.

## What is broken or risky, ranked for a polymer-clay user

The first group is silent wrong answers, where the UI accepts input and the output disagrees without a warning. Strand height is not honoured under high draft or narrow strands. With the requested 1.2 mm, a 0.4 mm strand reaches 0.55 mm at the default 20 degree draft, because the cone cap at the centreline is r·tan(90°−draft). The sliders allow width 0.4 and draft 45, and `check` does not compare achieved with requested height. The surface amplitude slider is a no-op: heights are min-max normalised to the relief field, so a=1 and a=3 give identical meshes. Hanging loops are silently ignored on the surface kind while the checkbox stays ticked. The base plate is a full rectangle that multiplies form volume by 7.5 on a 40 mm rose (269 to 2021 mm³), yet clay volume and ball counts use only the relief height. Two-sided mode silently ignores `base_mm`. Finally, "size" refers to the centreline, so a nominal 40 mm circle is 41.7 mm across, and rotation is applied before fitting, so a 3:1 ellipse's stroke length grows 34% at 45 degrees (all measured, [model_generation.md](file:///home/user/press-moulds/research_notes/Press%20mould%20and%20model%20assessment/model_generation.md)). At 20-40 mm, a 1.8 mm strand overshoot is a noticeable 4-9%.

The second group is geometric holes in the release guarantee. A negative `loop_shift_mm` forces the hole disc to zero height regardless of what it overlaps, leaving walls only 2.4 degrees from vertical on all 14 curve designs tested ([geometry.py:236-237](file:///home/user/press-moulds/equation-playground/geometry.py)). The draft slider accepts 0-45 degrees and draft 0 builds clean with no warning. For polymer clay pressed by hand into a rigid resin cavity, the draft is the main release aid, so these matter more than they would for a flexible silicone mould.

The third group is the Check report itself. `_thinnest` subsamples to 0.1 mm at the default pixel and reports in about 0.2 mm steps. A synthetic sweep gave true 0.5, 0.55 and 0.6 mm gaps reading 0.7 mm, so gaps in the fragile range pass the 0.6 mm warning, and the same design flips between 0.30, 0.55 and 0.45 mm as size or pixel change. With 20-degree draft the resin ridges between strands are posts supported at one end, so a 0.55 mm rim is plausibly the first thing to chip when shaving clay (inference). The lid is not checked for watertightness at all. On the fractal quilt surface design, lid simplification failed both targets and silently fell back to 3.7 million faces and a 185 MB STL. The simplifier's own acceptance test allows a 1% volume error, about 220 mm³ on a mould that holds a 700 mm³ cavity, though the actual error measured 0.1 mm³.

The fourth group is scale and memory. Time and memory grow with (size/pixel)², with no guard. At 0.02 mm and size 30 the mould build raised a `std::bad_alloc` under a 13 GB cap, and a 200 mm form at 0.02 mm was killed by the OOM killer on a 16 GB box. Both settings are inside the UI limits. For 20-40 mm earrings the slider extremes are reachable by accident but not needed. The live preview is more of a cosmetic issue at this size. Its pixel is max(px, 0.2, size/220), so height steps reach 0.55 mm on the 70-degree walls and look blocky, but a 1.8 mm strand still spans 9 pixels at 40 mm. The preview only stops resembling a strand above roughly 100 mm.

## What a polymer-clay jewellery maker still lacks

Release and pressing guidance is entirely absent. A grep for release, talc or undercut finds only the module docstring. External practice, read from snippets, is that silicone moulds usually need no release for polymer clay, with cornstarch or water as fallbacks, and that FIMO reportedly turns sticky with water ([Sculpey](https://www.sculpey.com/blogs/blog/using-silicone-moulds-with-polymer-clay)). The same Sculpey page warns against baking in a non-bakeable mould. Because cured printed resin is porous and rigid, a light cornstarch dusting plus a demould-before-baking step is the likeliest workflow, and the app should say so and mark it as untested. Nothing in the evidence tells us whether a standard printed resin survives 110 C. No source retrieved gives its heat deflection, so assume pieces are baked out of the mould unless the resin's datasheet says otherwise. The app also gives no printing-orientation advice. The mould is a 93 x 54 mm flat plate with a cavity face, a large peel area, and the external guidance is to tilt 30-45 degrees to soften layer lines ([Anycubic](https://wiki.anycubic.com/en/resin-3d-printer/anycubic-photon-p1-max/severe-layer-lines-troubleshooting-guide), snippet). Cured resin must be fully washed and post-cured before it touches clay ([Smooth-On FAQ](https://www.smooth-on.com/support/faq/210/), written about silicone inhibition).

Findings and design vocabulary are thin. The only attachment is a single ring loop (outer diameter 5, inner 1.8, height 1.5 mm). There is no stud or post pad, no side ear-wire hole, no double loop and no bezel. The strand has one width and one dome profile, the grammar allows one equation of one kind, and strands are always outlines, never filled shapes. The only multi-cavity layout is the mirrored left/right pair, so there is no n-up plate of mixed designs. There is no push-out notch or ejector, no clay-thickness stop, and registration pins sit at three corners only. Exports are binary STL plus SVG, with no 3MF units metadata and no importer for the saved `design.json`. A hand-pressing earring mould at 4.5 mm thick with a 1.5 mm cavity is thin for rolling and plausibly flexes (inference, no mechanical test). The clay calculator uses 1.7 g/cm³ labelled "polymer clay", which is the right default for this user ([clay.py:6](file:///home/user/press-moulds/equation-playground/clay.py)), though it also reports ball-zone errors up to 11% on a two-sided Lissajous and hits a 12-ball cap on the surface quilt.

Shrinkage is absent from both stages, and that is fine for FIMO Soft; the user's stated context gives negligible shrinkage, and the notes show no source contradicting it. It would matter if earthenware returns. The SIO-2 PA datasheet gives 6.8% drying and -0.4% firing at cone 04 (an unexplained slight expansion), and the compound formula 1 − (1−d)(1−f) gives about **6.4% total, a scale factor of 1/(1−T) about 1.068**, so a 30 mm piece must be pressed about 32 mm and the 1.8 mm hole fires to about 1.68 mm ([SIO-2 PA datasheet](https://www.ceramiq.pl/pliki/Karty/PA_us.pdf)). Drying and firing figures must not be added, since Digitalfire's example shows 6.25% plus 6.66% totalling 12.5%, not 12.9% ([Digitalfire](https://digitalfire.com/picture/2239), snippet). No cone 05 figure was found. Earthenware also gains risks the app does not address: resin is non-absorbent so wet clay sticks more than in plaster, and thin parts crack from uneven drying ([Ceramic Arts Network](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/daily/article/Persnickety-Porcelain-How-to-Minimize-Your-Loss-Rate-With-this-Gorgeous-Yet-Difficult-Material), snippet). The clay-density constant would also be wrong.

## How far the evidence reaches

The evidence quality is uneven, and the report's weight should follow it. The two code assessments are the strongest material: numbers were measured by running `geometry.py`, on a 4-core 16 GB machine, with tests and scratch scripts named. Their limits are stated. Nothing was printed, pressed or demoulded; browser render time and the stair-step look were inferred from hillshade images and pixel formulas, not a screenshot; effort estimates are judgement. The 15-design mould sample is small and tilted toward the author's own docs/earrings designs, and earring sizes were assumed to be 30 mm because that folder has no README.

The two external notes are weaker. `press_mould_best_practice.md` was read mainly from search summaries and says so; only the SIO-2 datasheet was read in full. Its draft figure (at least 2 degrees outside, 3 inside) comes from a single non-authoritative blog, and the notes found no sourced numbers for cavity depth, minimum strand width, gutter size, pin clearance or earthenware minimum thickness. Those rules remain tacit hobbyist knowledge. `model_generation_methods.md` is a snippet-level survey of alternatives (shapely buffer and extrude, profile sweep, SDF with marching cubes, 3MF export) with no benchmarks, and its method comparison table is flagged as unverified. One of its headline recommendations, to "stop binarising" and use a continuous distance profile, is already implemented, which the measured 0.037 mm height error confirms. Its AST-whitelist advice is also largely met, since the app uses `parse_expr` behind a whitelist and guards (SymPy's docs say `parse_expr` uses `eval` ([SymPy](https://eksamensvedlegg.it.ntnu.no/Felles/sympy-docs-html-1.14.0/modules/parsing.html))). That is acceptable for a local single-user tool and a risk only if hosted. Treat the methods note as a menu of options, not a verdict on the code.

## Prioritised fixes: cheap trust first

Effort figures are estimates from reading code, not implementations. Impact is weighted for FIMO earrings at 20-40 mm.

| Priority | Fix | Impact | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Replace the quantised thin-wall check with a full-resolution local-thickness map; warn below 0.6 mm plus one pixel and report the location | High: the main safety signal is wrong | Low-medium |
| 2 | Guard draft and loop: warn below 3-5 degrees, block or fix negative loop shift, test steepest wall after the loop is cut | High: release safety | Low |
| 3 | Warn when strand height is unreachable (r·tan(90°−draft) < h) and when surface amplitude is normalised; warn or disable the loop on surfaces | High: silent wrong answers | Low |
| 4 | Add pressing and release guidance (demould before baking, cornstarch dusting, slow lift, orientation tilt, wash and post-cure), with unverified items marked | High for this user | Trivial |
| 5 | Check the lid (watertight, face count, fallback warning) and tighten the 1% volume acceptance test | Medium | Low |
| 6 | Guard pixel x size (above about 6M cells refuse or warn); fit size after rotation; report size as outer extent (strand width included) | Medium | Low |
| 7 | Count the base plate in clay volume and balls; disable base when two-sided; hide `lid_t` in Full 3D | Medium | Low |
| 8 | Add `shrink_pct` (scale by 1/(1−s), fired size and hole in Check, clay-density selector) | Low now, high if earthenware returns | Low (about 10 lines) |
| 9 | Earring findings (stud pad, side wire hole), push-out notch, more pins, n-up plate, 3MF export, `design.json` import | Medium, wide | Medium-high |
| 10 | Tests for surface path, base and two-sided, loop angle and shift, rotate and mirror, draft cap | Medium | Small-medium |

The ordering follows the assessments' own: items 1-3, 5 and 6 total a few days and remove every silent error found. Preview smoothing, an adaptive implicit grid and a shapely or sweep geometry rewrite stay off this list, because at 20-40 mm the heightmap is already accurate to 0.04 mm.

## Conclusion

The assessment inverts the usual worry. The model is not where this app is weak; it is accurate to well under a print pixel, and the alternative-method literature mostly proposes things the code already does. The weakness lies in what the app tells the user: a Check report that rounds up, controls that quietly do nothing, and no pressing workflow. A maker starting with polymer clay is exposed to those trust gaps because nothing in the app's output signals when a mould is likely to chip or stick.

The largest unknown is physical, not computational. Nobody has yet pressed FIMO into one of these moulds, and no retrieved source gives press-mould draft, wall or gutter rules or the heat tolerance of printed resin. A single test print, one pair mould of a simple design at 30 mm, would calibrate the minimum resin wall, release technique and baking workflow faster than any further code review.
